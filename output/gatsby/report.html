📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves from the Midwest to West Egg, Long Island, to work in the bond business and rents a modest house next to the mysterious mansion of Jay Gatsby. There, he reconnects with his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom, wealthy East Eggers whose opulent life masks deep moral decay—Tom’s racism and infidelity, Daisy’s cynical detachment, and Jordan Baker’s dishonesty. Nick is drawn into their world, but it is Gatsby who captivates him: a self-made millionaire whose lavish parties mask an intense, quiet longing. Through Jordan Baker, Nick learns that Gatsby bought his mansion solely to be near Daisy, the woman he loved five years earlier as a young officer in Louisville, and that he has spent years crafting an elaborate persona—Oxford graduate, war hero, aristocrat—to win her back. Nick agrees to arrange a reunion between Gatsby and Daisy, reigniting their romance and exposing the fragility of Gatsby’s dream. As Gatsby and Daisy rekindle their relationship, tensions rise with Tom Buchanan, who grows suspicious and eventually confronts Gatsby in a brutal showdown at the Plaza Hotel. Tom dismantles Gatsby’s fabricated identity, revealing his criminal ties through Meyer Wolfsheim and exposing the source of his wealth as bootlegging. Daisy, unable to fully renounce Tom or declare she never loved him, shatters Gatsby’s idealized vision of their past. That same afternoon, Myrtle Wilson, Tom’s mistress, is killed by a speeding yellow car—driven by Daisy but owned by Gatsby. In the aftermath, Tom manipulates George Wilson, Myrtle’s widower, into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer. Meanwhile, Gatsby, still guarding Daisy’s house in the moonlight, refuses to leave, convinced he can protect her from blame. Nick, increasingly disillusioned by the East’s moral emptiness, watches as Gatsby’s world collapses around him, even as those who benefited from his hospitality vanish without a word. After Gatsby is shot and killed by George Wilson—who then turns the gun on himself—Nick struggles to organize a funeral for a man whose wealth and fame meant nothing to those who once flocked to his parties. Only Gatsby’s father, a humble Midwesterner who cherished his son’s ambition, and a few others attend. Nick confronts Jordan Baker, who dismisses his moral outrage, and encounters Tom Buchanan, who coldly admits he told Wilson about Gatsby’s car, justifying it as self-preservation. Haunted by the emptiness of Gatsby’s dream and the callousness of the wealthy, Nick leaves New York, realizing that people like Tom and Daisy are 'careless' people who smash things and retreat into their money. In his final reflection on the beach, Nick sees the green light at the end of Daisy’s dock as a symbol of the American Dream—endlessly pursued, always just out of reach, borne back ceaselessly into the past.

Themes: identity, ambition, loss
