📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, Long Island, seeking opportunity in the bond business and renting a modest home next to the lavish mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—a wealthy, arrogant Yale alumnus—Nick is introduced to the opulent yet morally hollow world of East Egg’s old money elite. He also meets Jordan Baker, a detached and dishonest golfer, who later reveals that Gatsby’s fortune and mansion were acquired solely to be near Daisy, his lost love from five years prior. Gatsby, who throws extravagant parties attended by strangers who know nothing of him, cautiously draws Nick into his orbit, confiding in him a carefully constructed but fabricated past of Oxford, European aristocracy, and wartime heroism, while secretly hoping Nick will arrange a reunion with Daisy. Nick agrees to facilitate a tea between Gatsby and Daisy, which reignites their romance and culminates in Gatsby’s belief that he can erase the past and recreate their lost love. As their affair deepens, tensions rise between Gatsby and Tom, who grows suspicious of Gatsby’s wealth and origins. During a sweltering afternoon in New York, the group confronts each other at the Plaza Hotel, where Tom exposes Gatsby’s ties to bootlegging through Meyer Wolfsheim and forces Daisy to admit she once loved both men—shattering Gatsby’s idealized vision. In the aftermath, Daisy, driving Gatsby’s yellow car, accidentally kills Myrtle Wilson, Tom’s mistress, who mistakes the car for Tom’s. Tom manipulates George Wilson, Myrtle’s grief-stricken husband, into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer, setting in motion a chain of violence. After the accident, Gatsby waits hopelessly by his pool for a call from Daisy, clinging to the fantasy that she will leave Tom. Meanwhile, George Wilson, misled by Tom, tracks Gatsby to his mansion and shoots him dead before taking his own life. Nick, left to organize Gatsby’s funeral, finds himself utterly alone: Daisy and Tom vanish without a word, Wolfsheim refuses to attend, and even Jordan Baker coldly ends their relationship. Only Gatsby’s impoverished father arrives, proud of his son’s ambition, revealed through a childhood book filled with self-improvement goals. As Nick reflects on the emptiness of the East and the corruption beneath its glittering surface, he concludes that Gatsby’s tragedy was not just in his death, but in his unwavering belief in a dream that could never be reclaimed—a dream embodied by the green light across the bay, forever out of reach, as all dreams are, beaten back by the relentless tide of time.

Themes: identity, ambition, loss
