📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, Long Island, seeking renewal after the war, renting a modest home next to the extravagant mansion of the enigmatic Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—a wealthy, arrogant Yale alumnus with a mistress, Myrtle Wilson—Nick is drawn into the opulent yet morally hollow world of East Egg’s elite. He also becomes acquainted with the cynical, detached Jordan Baker, and gradually forms a quiet fascination with Gatsby, whose lavish parties attract hundreds of anonymous guests yet conceal his solitary yearning. Nick learns from Jordan that Gatsby’s entire fortune and mansion were acquired with one purpose: to rekindle a lost romance with Daisy, whom he had met and loved five years earlier as a young officer before being sent overseas. As Nick becomes Gatsby’s confidant, he facilitates a reunion between Gatsby and Daisy, reigniting their connection amid Gatsby’s grand displays of wealth. But beneath the surface, Gatsby’s idealized vision of Daisy is fragile, built on illusions of class and status he fabricated to win her. Tom, suspicious of Gatsby’s background, confronts him during a tense trip to New York, exposing his bootlegging ties through Meyer Wolfsheim and dismantling his claims of Oxford pedigree. Daisy, overwhelmed, admits she once loved both men but refuses to renounce Tom. That same afternoon, Myrtle Wilson is killed by a speeding yellow car—driven by Daisy, though Gatsby takes the blame. In the aftermath, Tom manipulates George Wilson, Myrtle’s husband, into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer. Meanwhile, Gatsby waits in vain for Daisy to call, clinging to the hope she will choose him, even as she retreats with Tom into their insulated world of privilege and indifference. After Gatsby is shot and killed by George Wilson at his pool, Nick struggles to arrange a funeral, only to find that Gatsby’s so-called friends—those who feasted at his parties—vanish without a trace. Only Gatsby’s father, Henry C. Gatz, arrives from Minnesota, proud of his son’s achievements but unaware of the corruption behind them. Nick, disillusioned by the moral decay he has witnessed, rejects the East and resolves to return home. He breaks with Jordan Baker, who is now engaged to another man, and reflects on Gatsby’s tragic dream: the green light across the bay symbolized not just Daisy, but an impossible ideal of renewal and perfection that was already behind him. In the final moments, Nick stands alone on the beach, contemplating how humanity, like boats against the current, is forever borne back ceaselessly into the past.

Chapter 1: I 5,892 words · 39m

The chapter opens with Nick Carraway reflecting on his father’s advice to reserve judgment due to unequal advantages, a habit that has made him a confidant but left him disillusioned after returning from the East, where he witnessed moral chaos in privileged society—except for Jay Gatsby, whose extraordinary hope and romantic readiness he singles out as exceptional. In the spring of 1922, Nick rents a modest bungalow in West Egg for eighty dollars a month, acquiring an old Dodge, a dog (which soon runs away), and a Finnish housekeeper, while filling his shelves with twelve volumes on banking to cultivate intellectual and financial renewal. After feeling isolated, he gains a sense of belonging when a stranger asks him for directions to West Egg village; later, he drives by car to East Egg to dine with his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom, whom he knew at Yale, meeting the enigmatic Jordan Baker, who lies motionless on a couch. After Jordan reads aloud from the Saturday Evening Post and departs, Daisy teases Nick about marrying Jordan, while Tom coldly disapproves of her independence; Nick denies rumors he is engaged to a woman out West, explaining the gossip prompted his move East. As he drives home to West Egg, he pauses in his yard and sees Gatsby standing on the lawn, arms outstretched toward a distant green light across the bay, trembling before vanishing into the night.
