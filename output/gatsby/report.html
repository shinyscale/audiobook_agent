📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, Long Island, seeking opportunity in the bond business and renting a modest home next to the lavish mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy, arrogant residents of East Egg—Nick is introduced to the hedonistic world of the Jazz Age elite. He also meets Jordan Baker, a cynical professional golfer, and becomes fascinated by Gatsby’s enigmatic persona: a self-made millionaire known for extravagant parties attended by strangers who whisper rumors of his criminal past. Nick gradually learns that Gatsby’s entire fortune and mansion were acquired to rekindle a romance with Daisy, whom he loved five years earlier as a young officer before the war, when she married Tom Buchanan while he was away. Gatsby’s obsessive hope is centered on the green light across the bay, symbolizing his dream of recapturing the past. As Nick grows closer to Gatsby, he learns the truth behind Gatsby’s origins: James Gatz, a poor North Dakota farm boy who reinvented himself after working for the wealthy Dan Cody and absorbing the trappings of high society. Gatsby’s fabricated history—Oxford, war heroism, inherited wealth—is exposed as carefully constructed myth, yet his emotional sincerity remains undeniable. When Nick arranges a reunion between Gatsby and Daisy at his own home, their rekindled passion is palpable, but Daisy’s hesitation reveals the impossibility of Gatsby’s dream. The tension escalates during a sweltering day in New York, where Gatsby confronts Tom in a hotel room; Daisy’s wavering declaration that she once loved Tom shatters Gatsby’s idealized vision. On the drive home, Daisy—driving Gatsby’s yellow car—accidentally kills Myrtle Wilson, Tom’s mistress, who mistakes the vehicle for Tom’s. Instead of taking responsibility, Tom manipulates George Wilson, Myrtle’s grief-stricken husband, into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer. After the accident, Gatsby waits vigilantly outside Daisy’s house, determined to protect her, even as she retreats into the safety of Tom’s wealth. That night, George Wilson, consumed by delusion and manipulated by Tom, tracks Gatsby to his mansion and shoots him in the pool before turning the gun on himself. Nick, left to organize Gatsby’s funeral, finds that no one from the glittering crowd of partygoers attends—not even Meyer Wolfsheim or Klipspringer. Only Gatsby’s father, Henry C. Gatz, arrives, proud of his son’s rise but unaware of the corruption behind it. Nick confronts Jordan Baker, who ends their relationship, and encounters Tom on Fifth Avenue, where Tom coldly justifies his role in Gatsby’s death as self-defense. Disillusioned by the moral decay of the East, Nick returns to the Midwest, reflecting on Gatsby’s tragic belief that the past could be repeated. In the final moments, he watches the green light across the bay and contemplates humanity’s futile struggle against time: 'So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past.'
