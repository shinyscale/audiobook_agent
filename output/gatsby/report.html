📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg on Long Island, renting a modest home next to the lavish mansion of the enigmatic Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy, arrogant, and unfaithful—Nick is drawn into the orbit of East Egg’s privileged elite. He quickly becomes entangled in Gatsby’s world after receiving an invitation to one of the millionaire’s extravagant parties, where rumors swirl about Gatsby’s mysterious past. Nick forms a tentative connection with the cynical golf champion Jordan Baker, who later reveals that Gatsby’s entire fortune and estate were built in obsessive pursuit of Daisy, whom he loved five years earlier before being separated by war and class. Gatsby asks Nick to arrange a reunion, hoping to rekindle their romance and erase the intervening years, a dream fueled by his idealized memory of Daisy and the green light across the bay that symbolizes his unattainable longing. As Gatsby and Daisy rekindle their affair, tensions escalate between Gatsby and Tom, culminating in a violent confrontation at the Plaza Hotel where Tom exposes Gatsby’s criminal ties to Meyer Wolfsheim and ridicules his fabricated Oxford pedigree. Daisy, overwhelmed and conflicted, admits she once loved both men, shattering Gatsby’s illusion that they could return to the past. Later that afternoon, Daisy, driving Gatsby’s yellow car, accidentally kills Myrtle Wilson—Tom’s mistress—in a hit-and-run. Tom manipulates George Wilson, Myrtle’s grief-stricken husband, into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer. Meanwhile, Gatsby, still clinging to the hope that Daisy will leave Tom, waits outside her house in vain, taking the blame for the accident. That night, George Wilson, consumed by rage and misinformation, tracks Gatsby to his mansion and shoots him as he floats in his pool, then turns the gun on himself. In the aftermath, Nick Carraway is left alone to organize Gatsby’s funeral, only to find that the hundreds who once flocked to his parties have vanished. Daisy and Tom retreat into their wealth, leaving no trace of guilt; Meyer Wolfsheim refuses to attend, and Jordan Baker breaks off their relationship, revealing she is engaged to another. Only Gatsby’s father, Henry C. Gatz, arrives from Minnesota, proud of his son’s rise despite never knowing the truth behind it. Nick watches as the world moves on, disillusioned by the moral decay beneath the glittering surface of the East. Standing alone at Gatsby’s abandoned mansion, he reflects on the green light and the futile human struggle to recapture the past, concluding that we are all borne back ceaselessly into it. He leaves the East, rejecting its corruption, and returns to the Midwest, forever marked by the tragedy of a man who believed in a dream too beautiful for reality.

Themes: identity, ambition, loss

Chapter 1: I 5,892 words · 39m

The chapter opens with Nick Carraway reflecting on his father’s advice to reserve judgment, which shaped his tendency to absorb others’ confidences and ultimately led to his disillusionment after returning from the East, where he witnessed moral chaos and privileged excesses—except for Jay Gatsby, whose extraordinary gift for hope and romantic sensitivity set him apart. In the spring of 1922, Nick rents a modest eighty-dollar bungalow in West Egg, overlooking Long Island Sound, between two grand estates, including the mysterious mansion of Gatsby; after feeling isolated until a stranger asks him for directions, he drives by car to East Egg to visit his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom, whom he knew at Yale, where they live in a grand Georgian Colonial mansion with French windows open to the breeze. During dinner, Tom aggressively espouses racist pseudoscience from The Rise of the Coloured Empires, a phone call reveals his affair in New York, and Daisy, after retreating and returning with forced cheer, invents a story about a nightingale on the lawn before confiding in Nick that she wishes her daughter would be a 'beautiful little fool.' Later, as Nick leaves, he observes Gatsby standing alone on his lawn, stretching his arms toward a single green light across the bay, before vanishing into the night.
