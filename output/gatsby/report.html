📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, New York, seeking purpose in the bond business and renting a modest home beside the extravagant mansion of the enigmatic Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy, entitled residents of East Egg—Nick is introduced to a world of privilege and moral decay. He learns of Tom’s affair with Myrtle Wilson, a woman trapped in a loveless marriage in the desolate Valley of Ashes, and meets Jordan Baker, a cynical golf player who reveals Gatsby’s unspoken obsession with Daisy. One night, Nick witnesses Gatsby standing alone, reaching toward a green light across the bay, symbolizing his longing for a past he cannot reclaim. As Nick becomes entangled in Gatsby’s world, he learns through Jordan’s recounting of 1917 that Gatsby, once James Gatz, a poor North Dakota farm boy, reinvented himself after meeting Dan Cody and falling in love with Daisy during his brief military posting in Louisville, vowing to one day return to her as a man worthy of her world. Gatsby’s lavish parties, attended by nameless elites who spread wild rumors about him, serve as a stage for his performance of wealth meant to attract Daisy. Nick facilitates a reunion between Gatsby and Daisy at his own home, where Gatsby’s nervous grandeur—showing off his shirts, his mansion, and his carefully constructed identity—briefly rekindles their romance. But beneath the surface, cracks emerge: Tom Buchanan, suspicious and resentful, investigates Gatsby’s past and exposes him as a bootlegger tied to Meyer Wolfsheim, a mobster who fixed the 1919 World Series. The tension culminates in a violent confrontation at the Plaza Hotel, where Daisy, under pressure, admits she once loved Tom but cannot deny her feelings for Gatsby—yet refuses to renounce her marriage entirely. That night, Myrtle Wilson is killed by a yellow car driven by Daisy, though Gatsby takes the blame.
