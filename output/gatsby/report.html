📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, Long Island, seeking purpose in the finance world and renting a modest home next to the extravagant mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy, arrogant, and unfaithful—Nick is introduced to the glittering yet hollow world of East Egg’s elite. He also meets Jordan Baker, a cynical golf champion whose dishonesty mirrors the moral decay around them. Nick’s fascination with Gatsby begins when he witnesses him standing alone at night, arms outstretched toward a green light across the bay, symbolizing an unattainable dream. As Nick is drawn into Gatsby’s world through lavish parties and whispered rumors, he learns that Gatsby’s fortune and mansion were built solely to win back Daisy, whom he loved five years earlier before being separated by war and class. Gatsby’s past, revealed through Jordan Baker’s recollections and his own embellished tales, traces his transformation from James Gatz, a poor North Dakota farm boy, into the self-made millionaire Jay Gatsby, shaped by his time with the wealthy Dan Cody and fueled by an obsessive idealization of Daisy. Nick agrees to arrange a secret reunion between Gatsby and Daisy, which reignites their romance, but the illusion begins to crack under Tom’s hostility. At the Plaza Hotel, tensions erupt as Tom exposes Gatsby’s criminal ties through Meyer Wolfsheim and forces Daisy to admit she once loved Tom—and still does. The confrontation shatters Gatsby’s dream, and Daisy, overwhelmed, returns to Tom. That evening, Daisy, driving Gatsby’s car, accidentally kills Myrtle Wilson, Tom’s mistress, but Gatsby takes the blame to protect her. Meanwhile, Myrtle’s widower, George Wilson, misled by Tom into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer, descends into madness, fixated on the eyes of Doctor T. J. Eckleburg as divine judgment. After Gatsby’s death at Wilson’s hands—shot while floating in his pool—Nick is left to organize a funeral for a man whose world had no true friends. Only a handful attend: Gatsby’s father, a lonely minister, servants, the postman, and the owl-eyed man who marvels at the authenticity of Gatsby’s books. Nick’s desperate attempts to reach Daisy and Wolfsheim are met with cold indifference, and he learns Tom deliberately sent Wilson to Gatsby, knowing it would end in violence. Disillusioned by the carelessness of the wealthy, Nick realizes Gatsby’s dream was not just lost—it was doomed from the start by a society that values illusion over truth. In the final moments, Nick walks alone to Gatsby’s abandoned house, erases an obscenity on its steps, and gazes at the green light once more, reflecting on humanity’s futile struggle against time: 'So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past.'
