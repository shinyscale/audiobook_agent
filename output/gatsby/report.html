📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves from the Midwest to West Egg, Long Island, seeking opportunity in the bond business and renting a modest home beside the extravagant mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—a wealthy, arrogant Yale alumnus with a violent temper and a mistress, Myrtle Wilson—Nick is drawn into the glittering yet morally hollow world of East Egg’s elite. He soon becomes entangled in Gatsby’s obsessive quest to reunite with Daisy, whom Gatsby had loved five years earlier as a young officer before being sent to war. Gatsby, who fabricated an aristocratic identity from humble origins as James Gatz, bought his mansion solely so Daisy could see the green light at the end of her dock, and he enlists Nick’s help to arrange a private meeting. Their reunion is emotionally charged but reveals the gap between Gatsby’s idealized vision of Daisy and the flawed woman before him. As summer deepens, tensions rise between Gatsby and Tom, culminating in a volatile confrontation at the Plaza Hotel where Daisy, pressured by both men, admits she once loved both but cannot renounce her marriage. That afternoon, Myrtle Wilson is struck and killed by a speeding yellow car—Gatsby’s car—driven by Daisy, though Gatsby insists he will take the blame. George Wilson, Myrtle’s husband, consumed by grief and misled by Tom into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer, tracks Gatsby down. Meanwhile, Nick witnesses the moral collapse of those around him: Daisy and Tom retreat into their wealth after the tragedy, indifferent to the destruction they’ve caused; Gatsby’s so-called friends—including Meyer Wolfsheim and Klipspringer—vanish, refusing to attend his funeral; and Jordan Baker, whom Nick had grown close to, reveals her dishonesty and ends their relationship. Gatsby is shot dead by Wilson in his pool, and Wilson then takes his own life, leaving behind a silent, abandoned mansion. Nick Carraway is left to organize Gatsby’s funeral, but nearly everyone who once flocked to his parties refuses to come, revealing the emptiness of the social world Gatsby tried so desperately to join. Only Gatsby’s father, Henry C. Gatz, arrives, proudly displaying his son’s childhood self-improvement schedule, a testament to the disciplined dreamer beneath the facade. In the aftermath, Nick confronts Tom, who coldly admits he told Wilson about Gatsby’s car, justifying it as necessary protection. Disillusioned by the East’s moral decay and the careless cruelty of its inhabitants, Nick resolves to return home, reflecting on Gatsby’s unwavering belief in the future symbolized by the green light—how he reached for a dream that was always already behind him. The story ends with Nick’s haunting realization that humanity is perpetually borne back, ceaselessly, into the past.

Themes: identity, ambition, loss
