📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves from the Midwest to West Egg, Long Island, seeking opportunity in finance and renting a modest home next to the lavish mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy, entitled East Eggers—Nick is drawn into a world of excess and moral decay. He befriends the enigmatic Jordan Baker and is invited to Gatsby’s extravagant parties, where rumors swirl about Gatsby’s past. Gradually, Nick learns that Gatsby’s wealth and spectacle are all orchestrated to win back Daisy, whom he loved five years earlier as a young officer. Gatsby confides in Nick his dream of rekindling their romance, asking him to arrange a quiet meeting; when Daisy visits Gatsby’s home, the emotional reunion seems to reignite their connection, and Gatsby believes he can erase the intervening years and reclaim the past. As summer intensifies, tensions rise between Gatsby and Tom, who grows suspicious of Gatsby’s origins and wealth. During a heated confrontation at the Plaza Hotel, Daisy admits she once loved both men but cannot renounce her life with Tom, shattering Gatsby’s illusion. That afternoon, Daisy, driving Gatsby’s yellow car, accidentally kills Myrtle Wilson—Tom’s mistress—in the Valley of Ashes. Gatsby resolves to take the blame, clinging to his belief that Daisy will ultimately choose him. Meanwhile, Myrtle’s grieving husband, George Wilson, misled by Tom into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer, seeks vengeance. In a tragic culmination, Wilson shoots Gatsby in his pool and then turns the gun on himself, leaving the mansion silent and empty. In the aftermath, Nick Carraway struggles to organize Gatsby’s funeral, only to find that none of the hundreds who attended his parties care to attend—except for Gatsby’s father, a humble Midwestern man proud of his son’s achievements, and the owl-eyed man from the library, who quietly calls Gatsby 'the poor son-of-a-bitch.' Nick confronts Jordan Baker, who ends their relationship, and later meets Tom, who coldly admits he told Wilson where to find Gatsby, justifying it as protection. Horrified by their carelessness, Nick realizes the East has corrupted its inhabitants, and he returns to the Midwest, haunted by Gatsby’s futile dream of recapturing the past. Standing alone on the beach, he reflects on the green light Gatsby once reached for, and the relentless human struggle against time: 'So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past.'

Themes: identity, ambition, loss
