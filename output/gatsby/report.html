📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, New York, seeking opportunity in the bond business and renting a modest home next to the lavish mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy residents of East Egg—Nick is drawn into a world of excess and moral decay. He learns of Tom’s affair with Myrtle Wilson, wife of a mechanic in the desolate valley of ashes, and is invited to Gatsby’s extravagant parties, where rumors swirl about the host’s origins. Through Jordan Baker, Nick discovers Gatsby’s secret: he bought his mansion solely to be near Daisy, whom he loved five years earlier as a young officer before she married Tom. Gatsby enlists Nick’s help to reunite with Daisy, and their emotional reunion at Nick’s home reveals the depth of Gatsby’s idealized love and the fragility of his dream. As summer progresses, tensions escalate between Gatsby and Tom, culminating in a dramatic confrontation at the Plaza Hotel where Daisy, under pressure, refuses to renounce her marriage. That same day, Myrtle Wilson is killed by a speeding yellow car—driven by Daisy but owned by Gatsby—and her husband George, misled by Tom into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and her killer, descends into grief-fueled madness. Gatsby, steadfast in his devotion, takes the blame for the accident and waits vigilantly outside the Buchanan home, hoping Daisy will choose him. Meanwhile, Nick struggles to connect with the hollow elite around him, growing disillusioned by their carelessness and moral indifference, particularly after learning Jordan Baker cheated in golf and Tom lies with impunity. After Gatsby is shot and killed by George Wilson—who then takes his own life—Nick tries to organize a proper funeral but is met with silence from Gatsby’s so-called friends and associates. Only Gatsby’s father, Henry C. Gatz, arrives, revealing the humble Midwestern roots and relentless self-improvement that shaped Gatsby’s identity. As Nick reflects on the emptiness of the East and the corruption of the American Dream, he confronts Jordan Baker one last time, ending their relationship with sorrow, and later encounters Tom, who coldly admits to directing Wilson to Gatsby. Alone at Gatsby’s abandoned mansion, Nick gazes across the bay at the now-dimmed green light, realizing that Gatsby’s tragedy was not just lost love but the futile human struggle to recapture a past that can never be reclaimed. He departs the East, haunted by the image of boats beating against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past.
