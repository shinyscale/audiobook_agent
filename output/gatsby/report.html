📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg on Long Island, seeking opportunity in the bond business and renting a modest home next to the lavish mansion of the enigmatic Jay Gatsby. Through his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom—wealthy, arrogant, and unfaithful—Nick is introduced to the decadent world of East Egg’s old money elite, as well as the reckless excess of Gatsby’s Saturday night parties. He also becomes romantically entangled with Jordan Baker, a cynical professional golfer, while growing increasingly fascinated by Gatsby’s mysterious persona and his quiet obsession with Daisy. Gatsby, it emerges, is not the aristocrat he appears to be but James Gatz, a poor North Dakota farm boy who reinvented himself after meeting the wealthy yacht owner Dan Cody, shaping his entire life around the dream of reclaiming a lost love with Daisy from five years prior. As Nick becomes Gatsby’s confidant, he learns that Gatsby bought his mansion solely to be near Daisy’s dock, where a green light symbolizes his unattainable ideal. With Nick’s help, Gatsby reunites with Daisy in a tense, emotional meeting that reignites their connection, but the illusion begins to unravel when Tom Buchanan confronts Gatsby at the Plaza Hotel, exposing his bootlegging ties and undermining his social legitimacy. Daisy, unable to fully renounce Tom, admits she once loved both men, shattering Gatsby’s fantasy of erasing the past. The confrontation ends with Daisy driving Gatsby’s car back to Long Island, where she accidentally kills Myrtle Wilson, Tom’s mistress. Though Daisy is behind the wheel, Gatsby takes full blame, determined to protect her. Meanwhile, Myrtle’s grieving husband, George Wilson—led astray by Tom’s lie that Gatsby owned the car and was responsible for her death—tracks Gatsby down, believing him to be both a murderer and a lover who betrayed Myrtle. In the aftermath of Gatsby’s murder at his pool—shot by Wilson before Wilson turns the gun on himself—Nick Carraway is left to arrange a funeral for a man whose glittering world evaporates in death. Despite Gatsby’s countless party guests and acquaintances, nearly all abandon him: Jordan Baker breaks off their relationship, Meyer Wolfsheim refuses involvement, and even Klipspringer calls only to retrieve his tennis shoes. Only Gatsby’s father, Henry C. Gatz, arrives from Minnesota, proud yet heartbroken, revealing a childhood scrapbook filled with self-improvement goals that mirror Gatsby’s relentless ambition. As Nick reflects on the hollow morality of the East, he confronts Tom Buchanan, who coldly justifies his role in the tragedy, and finally walks alone along the beach, meditating on the green light Gatsby once reached for—the symbol of a dream as lost and unreachable as the original promise of America itself. Nick, disillusioned but alive, resolves to return to the Midwest, having witnessed the collapse of illusion beneath the weight of wealth and desire.

Themes: identity, ambition, loss
