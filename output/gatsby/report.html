📖 Plot Summary

In the spring of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, Long Island, and becomes entangled in the lives of his wealthy cousin Daisy Buchanan and her husband Tom, a brash Yale alumnus with a mistress in the desolate Valley of Ashes. Through Daisy, Nick meets the enigmatic Jordan Baker, and soon becomes fascinated by their mysterious neighbor, Jay Gatsby, whose lavish parties draw crowds but conceal his true identity. Nick learns that Gatsby’s fortune and mansion were acquired solely to rekindle a lost romance with Daisy, whom he met as a young officer in Louisville five years earlier. As Gatsby draws Nick into his world, he reveals a carefully constructed persona—Oxford-educated war hero, heir to immense wealth—though fragments of his past as James Gatz of North Dakota emerge, revealing a self-made man shaped by obsession and idealism. Nick, increasingly disillusioned by the moral decay of the East, finds himself uniquely moved by Gatsby’s extraordinary capacity for hope. The rising tension culminates in a carefully orchestrated reunion between Gatsby and Daisy at Nick’s home, where their emotional reconnection reignites Gatsby’s dream of reclaiming the past. But when Tom confronts Gatsby at the Plaza Hotel, the fragile illusion shatters: Daisy admits she once loved both men but cannot abandon Tom, and Gatsby’s fabricated identity is exposed as a product of bootlegging and criminal ties through Meyer Wolfsheim. The group departs in chaos, and shortly after, Myrtle Wilson is killed by a speeding yellow car—driven by Daisy, though Gatsby takes the blame to protect her. Tom, knowing the truth, directs the grieving Myrtle’s husband George Wilson to Gatsby, falsely implicating him as both Myrtle’s lover and killer. Meanwhile, Nick watches helplessly as Gatsby clings to the hope that Daisy will leave Tom, even as she retreats into the safety of her marriage, leaving Gatsby alone in the moonlight, guarding a house that no longer belongs to his dream. After Gatsby is shot and killed by Wilson in his pool, Nick struggles to organize a funeral, only to find that none of Gatsby’s so-called friends—Daisy, Tom, Wolfsheim, Klipspringer—will attend. Gatsby’s father arrives from Minnesota, proud of the son he barely knew, holding onto a photograph and a child’s schedule of self-improvement that reveals Gatsby’s lifelong pursuit of reinvention. At the sparse funeral, only a handful of people remain: Nick, Gatsby’s father, a few servants, and the mysterious owl-eyed man who quietly calls him 'the poor son-of-a-bitch.' Nick later confronts Tom, who admits he told Wilson about Gatsby’s car but feels no remorse, justifying it as necessary. Devastated by the emptiness of the world he has witnessed, Nick sells his car, abandons New York, and returns to the Midwest, reflecting on Gatsby’s dream as a metaphor for the American ideal—forever reaching toward a green light that recedes with every advance, boats beaten back ceaselessly into the past.

Themes: identity, ambition, loss
