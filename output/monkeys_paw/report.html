📖 Plot Summary

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert spend a quiet evening playing chess by the fire when Sergeant-Major Morris, an old friend returning from India, arrives unexpectedly. Over whiskey, Morris reveals the legend of a monkey’s paw—a cursed talisman capable of granting three wishes to three men, each at a terrible cost. After warning them of its deadly consequences and throwing the paw into the fire, Mr. White rescues it despite the soldier’s dire pleas. Herbert jokingly suggests wishing for two hundred pounds to pay off their debts, and Mr. White, half-laughing, makes the wish aloud—only to be jolted by a violent crash from the piano and the unsettling sensation that the paw twisted like a snake in his hand. The next morning, the family dismisses the event as coincidence, but that evening, a solemn stranger arrives with devastating news: Herbert has been killed in a machinery accident at work, and the company offers exactly two hundred pounds in compensation. The chilling precision of the amount shatters the Whites’ disbelief in the paw’s power, plunging them into profound grief. In the weeks that follow, Mr. and Mrs. White withdraw into silence, haunted by their loss and the unspoken horror of what they now believe the paw can do. Consumed by maternal desperation, Mrs. White demands her husband use the paw to bring Herbert back to life. Though terrified of the consequences—knowing his son’s body was horribly mangled—he relents and makes the second wish. As they sit in agonizing silence, a slow, then frantic, knocking begins at the front door. Mrs. White, convinced it is Herbert, rushes to open it, her hope overwhelming her terror. Mr. White, realizing what awaits—his son returned as a grotesque, mutilated thing—frantically searches the floor for the paw and makes a final, desperate third wish to undo the second. The knocking ceases instantly. The wind howls up the stairs, and Mrs. White lets out a heart-wrenching cry as her husband finds her at the door, alone in the darkness of an empty, deserted street, the paw now powerless and their grief complete.

Themes: loss, grief, the cost of desire

Over whiskey, Morris recounts tales of distant lands and reveals the existence of a monkey’s paw—a dried, mummified talisman enchanted by a fakir to grant three wishes to three different men, each at terrible cost. He confesses his own third wish was for death after his first two brought ruin, and he throws the paw into the fire; Mr. White rescues it despite the soldier’s dire warnings. The chapter opens on a bright winter morning at the White family home, where the father, Mr. White, dismisses his fears about the mysterious monkey's paw after laughing off its supposed powers during breakfast. His wife and son Herbert jest about the paw’s insignificance, tossing it carelessly onto the sideboard, while Herbert prepares to leave for work by walking down the road. Later, at dinner, Mr. White insists the paw moved in his hand the previous night, despite his wife’s attempts to soothe him. In the weeks following their son Herbert's death, the elderly couple retreats into silent grief in their shadowed house, two miles from the cemetery where he was buried. As they wait in oppressive silence, a stealthy knock sounds at the front door, escalating into violent pounding that the wife recognizes as Herbert — she rushes to open it, but the husband frantically searches the floor for the paw and makes a final, desperate third wish just as the door begins to open. The knocking stops abruptly, the wind rushes upstairs, and the wife lets out a heart-wrenching cry as the husband finds her at the door to an empty, deserted street.
