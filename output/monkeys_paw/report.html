📖 Plot Summary

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are enjoying a quiet evening by the fire when Sergeant-Major Morris, a weary veteran returning from India, arrives unexpectedly. Over whiskey, Morris reveals the existence of a monkey’s paw—a cursed talisman capable of granting three wishes to three men, each with horrific consequences. He confesses that his own third wish was for death and attempts to destroy the paw, but Mr. White retrieves it, and in a moment of reckless jest, Herbert urges his father to wish for two hundred pounds. The wish is spoken, the paw twists violently in Mr. White’s hand, and an unnatural silence descends, foreshadowing the tragedy to come. The next morning, the Whites dismiss the paw as nonsense, but their comfort shatters when a solemn stranger from Maw and Meggins arrives to deliver the news that Herbert has been killed in a machinery accident at work. In a chilling twist, the company offers exactly two hundred pounds in compensation—the precise amount Mr. White had wished for. Mrs. White’s grief quickly curdles into horrified realization that the paw’s magic is real and malevolent; Mr. White, overwhelmed by guilt and dread, collapses, his skepticism replaced by paralyzing fear as the supernatural force he once mocked now owns their fate. In the days that follow, the house becomes a tomb of silence and sorrow until Mrs. White, driven mad by grief, demands her husband use the paw to wish Herbert back to life. Terrified at the thought of his son returning as a mangled corpse, Mr. White reluctantly complies. The moment the wish is spoken, the house is swallowed by an unbearable quiet—then comes a slow, deliberate knock at the door. Mrs. White, wild with hope, rushes to open it, while Mr. White, in a final act of desperate mercy, finds the paw and makes his third wish: to undo the second. The knocking stops instantly. The door remains shut. The road outside is empty. The family is left with nothing but the chilling weight of their own hubris and the silence where their son once laughed.

Themes: loss, ambition, fate

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are playing chess by a warm fire when Sergeant-Major Morris, a veteran returning from India, arrives by foot after a long journey and is invited in. Over whiskey, Morris recounts his experiences and reveals the existence of a monkey’s paw, a mummified talisman enchanted by a holy fakir to grant three wishes to three separate men, each with dire consequences; he discloses that his own third wish was for death and that the paw has caused terrible misfortune. Despite his warnings, Mr. White retrieves the paw from the fire after Morris tries to destroy it, and after jesting about its power, Herbert suggests wishing for two hundred pounds. Mr. White makes the wish aloud, and the paw twists in his hand—though no money appears immediately, the atmosphere grows heavy with unease as the wind howls outside and a strange silence falls over the house.

In the quiet, shadowed house two miles from the cemetery, an elderly couple grieves silently after the death of their son, Herbert, until the wife, overcome by desperation, remembers the monkey’s paw and demands her husband use it to wish their son back to life. The old man, horrified by the prospect of reviving a mutilated corpse, reluctantly complies, and after the wish is spoken, the house falls into oppressive silence. Moments later, a stealthy knock sounds at the front door, and the wife, convinced it is Herbert, rushes to open it, while the husband frantically searches the floor for the paw to undo the wish.
