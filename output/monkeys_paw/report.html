📖 Plot Summary

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. White, his son Herbert, and Mrs. White play chess until they are visited by Sergeant-Major Morris, a traveler who reveals a dried monkey's paw capable of granting three wishes. Morris warns that fate rules lives and interference brings sorrow, and although he attempts to destroy the talisman, Mr. White rescues it from the fire. Pressed by Herbert to wish for two hundred pounds to clear the house's debt, Mr. White makes the wish aloud as the paw twists like a snake, causing him to cry out while Herbert dismisses the movement as fancy despite seeing a simian face in the dying fire. The lighthearted atmosphere of the next morning shatters when a stranger from the firm Maw and Meggins arrives to deliver news that Herbert has been caught in machinery and is dead. The visitor informs the grieving parents that the firm disclaims all liability but offers a compensation sum of exactly two hundred pounds, matching the earlier wish. Upon hearing the specific amount, Mrs. White lets out a shriek while Mr. White collapses into a senseless heap on the floor, overwhelmed by the horror of their son's death and the supernatural connection to the compensation. In a shadowy house, the grieving parents are awakened by Mrs. White's desperate realization that they can use the remaining two wishes to bring Herbert back to life. Despite Mr. White's horror that the son's body was too mutilated to recognize, he retrieves the talisman and, under his wife's frantic urging, wishes for Herbert's resurrection, feeling immediate relief when the candle extinguishes. A quiet knock at the front door prompts the woman to believe Herbert has returned, but the old man, terrified of what might enter, frantically searches for the paw while his wife struggles to unbolt the door. As the knocking intensifies, the old man finds the talisman and breathes his third wish, causing the noise to cease instantly before the woman discovers the street outside is quiet and deserted.

Themes: fate, consequences, grief, greed
