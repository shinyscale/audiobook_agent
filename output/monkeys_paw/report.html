📖 Plot Summary

The story begins at Laburnam Villa where Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are visited by Sergeant-Major Morris, who brings a cursed monkey's paw from India. Despite Morris's warnings that the paw brings sorrow to those who interfere with fate, Mr. White retrieves the talisman after Morris attempts to destroy it. Herbert jests about wishing for money, prompting Mr. White to wish for two hundred pounds, causing the paw to twist violently and a crash to occur from the piano, while Herbert later sees a horrifying simian face in the fire. The following morning, the family mocks the paw's magic before Herbert leaves for work, only to receive a devastating visitor from the firm Maw and Meggins. The stranger confirms that Herbert has been killed in machinery at his workplace and offers two hundred pounds as compensation, matching the exact amount Mr. White wished for. This news causes Mr. White to faint and Mrs. White to shriek, shifting the tone from domestic levity to devastating grief as the family buries their son. Consumed by grief, Mrs. White demands the paw be used to wish their son alive again, leading Mr. White to retrieve the talisman and make the wish despite his fears that the boy is too mutilated to be recognized. A stealthy knock at the door follows, causing the wife to scream that Herbert has returned while the husband frantically searches for the paw to make a final wish. As the bolt draws back and a cold wind rushes in, Mr. White breathes his third wish to stop the knocking, leaving the street quiet and deserted.

Themes: fate, consequences, grief

Chapter 1 1,742 words · 11m

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. White, his son Herbert, and Mrs. White are playing chess when they are visited by Sergeant-Major Morris, a former soldier who recounts wild tales from India and introduces a cursed monkey's paw that grants three wishes to three separate men. Despite Morris's grave warnings that the paw brings sorrow to those who interfere with fate and his attempt to destroy it by throwing it into the fire, Mr. White retrieves the talisman and, encouraged by Herbert's jesting about clearing their house, wishes for two hundred pounds. The paw twists violently in Mr. White's hand like a snake, causing a shuddering cry and a crash from the piano, yet no money immediately appears, leaving the family in an uneasy silence as they retire for the night. Later, alone in the darkness, Herbert sees a horrifying simian face in the dying fire, which vanishes only when he touches the monkey's paw, filling him with unease before he goes to bed.

Chapter 3: 3 1,276 words · 8m

In a house steeped in shadow two miles from a new cemetery, an old couple transitions from grief-stricken expectation to resignation after burying their son, until the wife wakes one night hysterically demanding the monkey's paw to wish their son alive again. Despite the husband's terrified warnings that the son was too mutilated to recognize, he retrieves the talisman and, under his wife's frantic pressure, wishes for the boy's return, only to be filled with unspeakable relief when nothing immediately happens. Later, a quiet, stealthy knock at the front door in the darkness prompts the wife to scream that it is Herbert, causing the husband to desperately search the floor for the paw while she struggles to unlock the door. As the bolt slowly draws back and a cold wind rushes up the staircase, the husband frantically breathes his third and final wish, causing the knocking to cease instantly and leaving the street outside quiet and deserted.
