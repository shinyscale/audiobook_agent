📖 Plot Summary

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, the White family—Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert—are visited by Sergeant-Major Morris, an old friend who brings with him a mysterious monkey’s paw said to grant three wishes to three different men. Morris, haunted by the paw’s dark legacy—its previous owner used his final wish to end his life—warns them of its terrible cost, but Mr. White, intrigued and lightly spurred by Herbert’s jesting enthusiasm, takes the paw. That night, against Morris’s dire advice, he wishes for two hundred pounds. The next morning, the family laughs off the incident as superstition, unaware that their lives are about to shatter. The chilling confirmation of the paw’s power arrives when a solemn stranger arrives on foot to deliver the news that Herbert has been killed in a machinery accident at work, and the company is offering exactly two hundred pounds in compensation as a settlement. The Whites are devastated, and the once-dismissed talisman now looms as a horrifying instrument of fate. A week later, consumed by grief and desperation, Mrs. White insists Mr. White use the paw’s second wish to bring Herbert back to life. Though trembling with dread, he complies, and as the night deepens, an eerie silence falls—until faint, deliberate knocks begin at the front door. Mrs. White, wild with hope, rushes to open it, convinced her son stands outside. In a moment of primal terror, Mr. White realizes what has been unleashed: whatever returns will not be their son, but a mutilated, unnatural thing forged by the paw’s curse. Frantic and sobbing, he scrambles for the paw on the floor, his fingers closing around it as Mrs. White pulls back the final bolt. With a final, desperate wish, he begs for Herbert to remain dead—and instantly, the knocking ceases. The door opens to reveal only the howling wind and an empty, rain-slicked road. The paw lies cold and still in Mr. White’s hand, its power spent, its price paid in unspeakable loss. The family is left with nothing but silence, grief, and the chilling knowledge that some doors should never be opened.

Themes: loss, ambition, fate
