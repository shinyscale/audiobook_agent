📖 Plot Summary

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are enjoying a quiet evening playing chess by the fire when Sergeant-Major Morris, an old friend returning from India, arrives unexpectedly. Over whiskey, Morris reveals the existence of a mysterious monkey’s paw—a cursed talisman capable of granting three wishes to three men, each with terrible consequences. Despite his warnings and attempt to destroy it, Mr. White retrieves the paw, and in a moment of lighthearted skepticism, Herbert jokingly suggests wishing for two hundred pounds. Mr. White makes the wish, and the paw twists violently in his hand, followed by an unsettling silence that lingers long after the night ends. The next morning, the family dismisses the incident as mere superstition, but the eerie atmosphere returns when a formal visitor from Maw and Meggins arrives to deliver devastating news: Herbert has been killed in a workplace accident, and the company offers exactly two hundred pounds in compensation. The uncanny precision of the sum shatters the Whites’ denial; Mr. White collapses, his faint smile revealing a horrifying realization that the paw’s power is real. In the week following their son’s burial, grief consumes the household, with Mrs. White descending into desperate hope and Mr. White sinking into silent despair, their once-close bond frayed by unspoken terror. Overcome by maternal longing, Mrs. White demands her husband use the paw to bring Herbert back to life. Horrified at the thought of confronting his son’s mutilated remains, Mr. White reluctantly complies, making the wish in the darkened parlor. Instantly, a series of ominous knocks echo at the front door. Mrs. White, consumed by delirious hope, rushes to open it, while Mr. White, in a final act of agonizing clarity, frantically searches for the paw to undo the wish. He finds it just as the last bolt is drawn and makes his third wish—silence falls, the knocking stops, and a long, heart-wrenching wail of despair echoes through the house, leaving the couple utterly alone with the weight of their terrible choice.

Themes: loss, ambition, fate

Chapter 1: I. 1,726 words · 11m

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are playing chess by a fire when Sergeant-Major Morris arrives by train, having just returned from India. Over whiskey, Morris recounts tales of distant lands and reveals the existence of a monkey’s paw, a mummified talisman enchanted by a fakir to grant three wishes to three different men, each with dire consequences — the previous owner wished for death as his third wish. Despite Morris’s warnings and his attempt to burn the paw, Mr. White retrieves it, and after jesting about its power, Herbert suggests wishing for two hundred pounds. Mr. White makes the wish aloud, and the paw twists in his hand, causing a shock and unease; the family dismisses it as imagination, but an eerie silence falls over the house as the night deepens.

Chapter 2: II. 936 words · 6m

The chapter opens on a bright winter morning at the White family home, where the father, Mr. White, dismisses his fears about the mysterious monkey's paw after laughing off its supposed powers during breakfast. His wife and son Herbert jest about the paw’s insignificance, tossing it carelessly onto the sideboard, while Herbert departs for work on foot. Later, at dinner, Mr. White insists the paw moved in his hand, despite his wife’s reassurances. Their conversation is interrupted by the arrival of a well-dressed stranger who hesitates at the gate before entering the house; Mrs. White nervously hides her apron under a chair. The visitor, representing the firm 'Maw and Meggins,' delivers the news that Herbert has been killed in a machinery accident at work, and though they disclaim liability, they offer two hundred pounds as compensation. Upon hearing the exact amount, Mr. White collapses unconscious, his faint smile betraying a chilling recognition of the paw’s supernatural fulfillment.
Characters: Mr. White Mrs. White Herbert the stranger

Chapter 3: III. 1,276 words · 8m

A week after burying their son, an elderly couple retreats into silent grief in their shadowed home until the wife, overcome by desperate hope, demands the monkey’s paw be retrieved to wish their dead son back to life. The husband, horrified by the prospect of confronting his mutilated corpse—revealed earlier only by clothing—reluctantly consents and makes the wish in the dark parlour. As the candle flickers out, a series of stealthy knocks sound at the front door, and the wife, convinced it is her son, rushes to open it while the husband frantically searches the floor for the paw to undo the wish. He finds it just as the final bolt is drawn, and makes his third and final wish as the knocking ceases and a long wail of despair echoes through the house.
