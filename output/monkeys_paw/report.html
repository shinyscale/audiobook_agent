📖 Plot Summary

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are enjoying a quiet evening playing chess by the fire when Sergeant-Major Morris, an old friend returning from India, arrives unexpectedly. Over whiskey, Morris reveals the existence of a monkey’s paw — a cursed talisman capable of granting three wishes to three men, but at a terrible cost, as its previous owner wished for death. Despite Morris’s warnings and his attempt to destroy the paw, Mr. White retrieves it, and under Herbert’s teasing provocation, he makes a careless wish for two hundred pounds. The paw twists violently in his hand, and an unsettling silence falls over the house, planting the first seed of dread. The next morning, the family dismisses the incident as superstition, laughing off the paw’s power and leaving it forgotten on the sideboard. But that evening, a somber stranger from Herbert’s workplace arrives with devastating news: Herbert has been killed in a machinery accident, and the company offers exactly two hundred pounds in compensation — the precise amount Mr. White wished for. The horrifying coincidence shatters the family’s sense of reality; Mr. White collapses in shock, while Mrs. White is consumed by grief. The once-lighthearted home now hangs heavy with unspoken terror, as the supernatural force they dismissed begins to exact its price. A week later, overwhelmed by inconsolable sorrow, Mrs. White demands her husband use the monkey’s paw to wish their son back to life. Terrified but powerless against her despair, Mr. White reluctantly makes the wish. As the night deepens, a slow, creeping knock begins at the front door — growing louder, more frantic — until it becomes violent pounding. Mrs. White, driven by desperate hope, rushes to open it, while Mr. White, realizing the horror of what awaits — a mangled, unnatural return — frantically searches for the paw. He finds it just as the final bolt is drawn and whispers his third and final wish. The knocking stops instantly. The house falls into an icy, suffocating silence, leaving the Whites alone with the unbearable weight of what they’ve unleashed and what they’ve lost.

Themes: loss, ambition, fate
