📖 Plot Summary

On a stormy night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert are visited by Sergeant-Major Morris, who brings a cursed monkey's paw capable of granting three wishes but bringing sorrow. Despite Morris's desperate warnings and attempt to destroy it, Mr. White insists on keeping the talisman. When Herbert jokingly suggests wishing for two hundred pounds, Mr. White makes the wish, causing the paw to twist violently in his hand like a snake while a horrible simian face appears in the dying fire. The following morning, the family dismisses the supernatural events until a stranger from Herbert's firm arrives to deliver tragic news. Herbert has been killed in a machinery accident, and the firm offers two hundred pounds as compensation for his services. As the horrific truth dawns on Mrs. White, Mr. White smiles faintly at the precise sum before collapsing senseless, leaving his wife shrieking in grief. After burying their son, the grieving wife demands the paw to bring Herbert back to life, forcing a terrified Mr. White to retrieve the talisman and make the second wish despite warnings of his son's mutilated state. A stealthy knock at the door convinces the wife that Herbert has returned, prompting her to struggle toward the entrance while her husband frantically searches for the paw to make a final wish. He breathes the third wish just as the door opens, causing the knocking to cease instantly and leaving only a cold wind and his wife's long wail of disappointment.

Themes: fate, grief, consequences

Chapter 1: 1 1,734 words · 11m

On a cold, wet night at Laburnam Villa, Mr. White, his son Herbert, and Mrs. White play chess when Sergeant-Major Morris arrives by foot through the stormy weather, bringing tales of India and a dried monkey's paw cursed by a holy fakir to grant three wishes to three men. Despite Morris's grave warnings that the paw causes sorrow and his attempt to burn it, Mr. White insists on keeping the talisman, eventually making a wish for two hundred pounds after Herbert jokingly suggests it. The paw twists violently in Mr. White's hand like a snake, causing a shock that unsettles the family, though no money immediately appears. Later, Herbert jokes about the money arriving in a bag while a horrible simian face appears in the dying fire, causing him to shiver as he goes to bed.

The chapter opens on a bright wintry morning at the White household, where Mr. and Mrs. White and their son Herbert dismiss the supernatural powers of the monkey's paw as nonsense before Herbert departs on foot for work. A well-dressed stranger from the firm 'Maw and Meggins' arrives at the gate, hesitating before entering the room to deliver news that Herbert has been badly hurt in a machinery accident. Upon realizing the stranger implies Herbert is dead, Mrs. White is momentarily relieved he feels no pain before the horrific truth dawns on her, leaving her breathless and white-faced while Mr. White stares blankly out the window. The visitor explains the firm disclaims all liability but offers two hundred pounds as compensation for Herbert's services, a sum that causes Mr. White to smile faintly and collapse senseless to the floor while Mrs. White shrieks.

Chapter 3: 3 1,276 words · 8m

In a house steeped in shadow two miles from the new cemetery, an old couple transitions from expectation to resignation after burying their son, until the wife wakes in the night demanding the monkey's paw to wish him alive again. The terrified old man descends into the dark parlour to retrieve the talisman, and despite his warnings about his son's mutilated state, he reluctantly makes the second wish while his wife waits with unnatural intensity. As the candle expires and silence returns, a quiet, stealthy knock at the front door convinces the wife that their son Herbert has returned, prompting her to struggle toward the door while her husband frantically searches the floor for the paw. In a panic, the husband locates the talisman and breathes his third and final wish just as the door opens, causing the knocking to cease instantly and leaving only a cold wind and a long wail of disappointment from his wife.
